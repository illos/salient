# Table command research: targeting and continuing resolution

Status: source research and proposed representation implications, 2026-09-11. No command syntax, storage schema, timing UI, or new product ruling is selected here. This note supplements the broader command inventory; it is not a complete combat specification or an automation-coverage claim.

All game citations below refer to the local Steel Compendium at **`fb83a789da8f0327a389c277a0c790b1648d5810`**. Each cited entry's SCC was checked for `mcdm.heroes.v1` or `mcdm.monsters.v1`. The unified tree also contains supplements: neither path presence nor a familiar class/monster name establishes core eligibility. Book-specific Heroes clean text supplies surrounding ability-resolution context; it is another representation of this corpus, not independent confirmation.

“Source rule” columns state the researched game behavior. “Needed input,” “derived fact,” and “continuation implication” are **research recommendations for what shared operations must be able to express**, not adopted command grammar. An input can come from existing state, a map adapter, a participant's supplied fact, or a recorded manual resolution. It need not be a typed argument on the first invocation.

## Roll, effect, and choice timing

| Example | Source rule | Needed input or choice | Derived fact | Continuation implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shared roll, different results | One roll applies to a multitarget ability, but target-local edges/banes can change tiers. The source example's total 11 becomes 13 against a target with an edge, giving tier 2 there and tier 1 elsewhere. [10][49] | Common dice/characteristic; actor-wide and target-local modifiers with provenance. | A tier per target, tied to one roll identity. | Never reroll separately per target merely because modifiers differ. Retain raw dice and intermediate totals for later rerolls/corrections. |
| Damage, then effects; Muse of Fire | Unless overridden, deal roll damage to all targets before tier effects. Multiple effects resolve in presented order. An effect on the actor/Director occurs once; with different target tiers, actor chooses the applicable tier. Muse of Fire reduces Malice by 2/3/4 and may make it negative. [11][25] | Per-target damage; actor choice among applicable effect tiers if needed. | All damage changes followed by one Malice reduction. | A naive loop of complete target resolutions is wrong: it interleaves damage/effects and can multiply one global effect. Do not silently choose the highest tier for the actor. |
| Potency, equality and conditional spending | A potency effect requires target characteristic strictly below the potency. A Heroic Resource or Malice spend whose entire effect depends on a resisted potency is not spent; a spend with an additional automatic effect still is. [13] | Target characteristic or authorized adjudicated comparison; potency modifiers; selected spend. | Independent damage and condition outcomes; whether selected cost is actually spent. | A failed potency is not a missed attack. Do not commit an unconditional cost for a potency-only option before its applicability is resolved. |
| Surges distributed by purpose | Up to 3 surges increase rolled damage to one target. Two surges increase potency by 1 for one target; additional pairs can increase potency for other targets, with no more than +1 per target from surges. [14] | Damage beneficiary and surge count; separate potency beneficiaries/counts. | Target-specific changes and total resource use. | One scalar “surges spent” loses the allocation. Resource options can arise at the damage event, not solely at ability declaration. |
| Trigger changes damage options: Advanced Tactics | An ally dealing damage gains 2 surges usable on that triggering damage; 1 focus can increase associated potency by 1. [36] | Triggering damage identity, responding tactician, optional focus, ally's new surge allocation. | Newly available resources and potency change for the pending effect. | Await the relevant reaction before treating damage/potency as final; the resources did not exist at initial declaration. |
| My Life for Yours: payer and beneficiary differ | On self/ally starting a turn or taking damage, censor spends their own Recovery and heals target by the censor's recovery value. Optional wrath ends one qualifying effect or lets a prone target stand. [42] | Censor, event target, optional wrath and selected effect/stand choice. | Spend from one actor and restoration to another. | Never infer Recovery payer or recovery value from healing recipient alone. |
| Again: explicit after-roll reroll | Target self/creature making an ability roll; after seeing its result, force a reroll and use the new roll. [34] | Triggering roll, responder and decision after dice are visible. | Replacement roll, with old roll retained as historical input. | A command can pause with known dice and unresolved result acceptance. No advance blanket reroll choice is required by this source. |
| Turnabout Is Fair Play: modifier replacement | Changes edge/double edge/bane/double bane on an ability roll, with a different transformation for 3 drama. [35] | Triggering roll and modifier state; optional drama. | Transformed modifier state, not just a numeric +2/-2 field. | Preserve modifier categories and their sources. Exact interaction with mixed modifiers on multiple targets remains a bounded interpretation question below. |
| Clever Trick, Meat Shield and No.: target replacement | Clever Trick redirects an enemy strike to an enemy within its distance, including its originator. Goblin Monarch's Meat Shield redirects a strike to an adjacent ally. Orc Rampart's No. substitutes itself for an adjacent ally if not already targeted. [31][45][46] | Triggering ability and affected original target role; chosen replacement where offered; reaction actor. | Replacement damage/effect recipient for the pending ability. | Record both original and replacement. These triggers differ in allowed attack types, relation and distance; do not treat them as one universal taunt operation. |
| Lines of Force: movement substitution | When a target would be force moved, choose a same-size-or-smaller new target within Melee 1. Fury becomes source, selects destination, may change the movement to push, and adds Might (twice Might for 1 ferocity). [32] | Original movement, original/new targets and size; new source; push option; destination; spend. | A replacement spatial effect with changed source, target, type and distance. | “Redirect to X” is insufficient unless the continuation can also acquire and apply these choices. |
| Sacred Bond: damage is not the whole outcome | Self and ally bond. When one takes damage, the other can use a free triggered action to take it instead; original target suffers associated effects. A Recovery spend can also trigger the partner's optional Recovery spend. [41] | Bond instance; triggering damage or Recovery event; partner's response. | Damage recipient and condition/effect recipient can diverge. | Do not implement all redirection as target replacement. Keep transferred damage tied to its original ability while preserving original associated effects. |
| Testudo!: moving before damage | Kobold Centurion reacts to an ability targeting it or an ally within distance. Allies in its 5 burst shift up to 2 before damage resolves; Shield? Shield! kobolds gain immunity 2 against that ability. [47] | Reaction's own burst membership; each shift; relevant trait holders; triggering ability identity. | New positions and temporary immunity scoped to one incoming ability. | Reactions can mutate geometry and mitigation before damage. This text does not itself establish a universal rule that leaving an area cancels an already targeted effect. |
| Damage immunity/weakness after reactions | Apply highest relevant weakness, then highest relevant immunity. Immunity is after other damage changes: the source's 8 damage halved to 4 then immunity 5 yields 0. [19][20] | Relevant per-recipient mitigation and the resolved earlier modifiers. | Actual Stamina loss differs across targets with the same base damage. | Do not freeze mitigation at initial ability declaration or show a provisional value as applied damage. |
| Hammer and Anvil: ally actions and late retarget | Tier determines one/two allies within 10 and possible edge; those allies use strike signature abilities as free triggered actions. If original target reaches 0 before a strike, that ally may select another target. [38] | Chosen allies; each ally's chosen signature ability and inputs; later replacement target if offered. | Dependent child abilities after the triggering ability's damage. | Recipient selection is tier-dependent. Preserve each ally's pending action and reassess the explicit retarget condition when it acts. |
| Double Strike: split use with retained roll | On user's turn, resolve against one target, then use that turn's maneuver and move action, then resolve against the second target using the same power roll. [39] | First target, Might/Agility choice, roll, intervening operations, second target. | One ability use/roll spanning other actions and changed spatial state. | The continuation must survive interleaving. Do not require immediate consecutive target resolutions or roll twice. Target selection/validation may be deferred until its stage. |
| Their Lack of Focus Is Their Undoing: actions before the parent roll | Three enemies each use a signature ability against targets chosen by the initiating actor, automatically tier 3. After those abilities resolve, the actor makes one shared power roll against the original targets for potency-gated dazed. [40][10] | Original enemies; their signature ability choices; their secondary targets and ability-specific choices; later parent roll. | Separate original/secondary target roles and child effects; an automatic tier is not fabricated dice. | Resolve child abilities before the parent potency roll. The actor, executing creature and choice-maker may be different entities. Generic multitarget rule still supplies one shared parent roll, not one per enemy. |
| Overwatch: mid-path interruption | Trigger is target movement; an ally can free strike at any point during it. Optional 1 focus slows on R below average; failed potency-only spend is not consumed. [37][13] | Target movement event, interruption point, ally and free-strike inputs, optional focus. | Pending movement may acquire a condition mid-operation. | A move cannot always be an indivisible endpoint update. Continue only from resolved intervening effects; precise remaining-movement behavior belongs in movement rules review. |
| Competing triggered/free triggered actions | One triggered action per round; free triggered actions do not use this allowance but share prohibitions. For the same trigger, player-controlled creatures decide order among themselves, then Director decides order for controlled creatures. [12] | Trigger identity; willing responders; control side and chosen order; allowance state. | Ordered reactions with their actual costs/prohibitions. | Need a pending event plus several optional responses, not a single mandatory reaction target. The source supplies side/order principles, not a complete software stack or timeout policy. |

## Persistent effects and minion squads

| Example | Source rule | Needed input or choice | Derived fact | Continuation implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Persistent Magic and Storm of Sands | Decide whether to maintain immediately after first use. Maintenance reduces essence earned at turn start by persistent value; cannot make it negative. Stop anytime without action; all active persistent abilities end at encounter end. Taking at least 5 × Reason damage in one turn stops all maintenance. Storm of Sands can persist, move up to 5 without action, and repeat its roll as a maneuver. [43][44] | Maintenance choice per active ability; expected essence income; cumulative damage this turn; later movement/repeat choices. | Maintained instances, resource-income changes, renewal and interruption. | This is the supported core mechanism; do not import a generic concentration slot/check. Same maintained ability on several targets rolls once and a creature cannot receive multiple instances. Track turn-scoped damage, not only the largest damage packet. |
| Duration and duplicate effects | Same-ability unique effects do not stack; most impactful applies and most recent use determines duration. Different effects imposing the same condition do not double the condition. A creature can normally end its imposed ability effect as a free maneuver. [10] | Effect identities, source uses, affected targets and durations; voluntary end choice. | Active effect contributions versus displayed aggregate condition. | A condition flag alone loses independent sources/durations. Ending one effect should not silently erase other continuing sources. |
| Minions attacked by an area | Squads share Stamina. Each minion's incoming damage contributes, but ordinary area damage kills only affected minions; source example caps 6 fire to each of three Stamina-5 goblins at 15 squad loss. Weakness/immunity are applied once to squad last and can drop/save multiple minions even for an area. [48] | Individual targets and squad memberships; current pool; affected membership; relevant squad modifiers. | Aggregate pool change plus particular casualties, with area and immunity/weakness rules. | Do not multiply squad immunity per target or process minions as independent full-Stamina actors. Keep casualty identities because later triggers target particular defeated creatures. |
| Minions attacked by non-area damage | Non-area damage can kill extra minions: initial damaged minions first, then nearest others. When several targets contribute damage crossing one threshold, damage dealer chooses which affected minion dies. Removal counts as reaching 0 for triggers. [48] | Pool, hit minions, distances/nearest candidates and any required casualty choice. | Additional deaths can occur outside original target list. | The result needs affected entities beyond declared targets. Missing nearest/tie facts require a supplied fact or adjudication, not random hidden selection. |
| Minion squad attacking | One roll for the squad signature ability. Each target receives one instance; up to three simultaneous attackers on a target add free-strike damage for additional minions. Source example: three pitlings split one/two across heroes deal 4 and 6 poison at tier 2. [48] | Participating minions, target assignment per minion, signature, shared roll and modifiers. | Per-target grouped damage and single condition instance; crit grants each participant an extra main action. | A squad actor name plus an unordered set of victims loses attacker assignment and participation. Keep group roll identity and individual entities. |
| Minion simultaneous free strikes | Several same-squad minions free striking the same target at once add damage and count as one strike. [48] | Squad membership, same event/target, participating minions. | One strike with combined damage. | Do not infer a separate trigger opportunity for every minion's damage as though these were independent strikes. |

## Invariants for later command design

These are proposed representation requirements inferred from the cases, constrained by already-confirmed product philosophy.

1. **Separate identities and roles.** Acting user, rules actor, responder, choosing participant, resource payer, original target, replacement target, damage recipient, effect recipient, beneficiary, and spatial origin can differ. A readable label is not enough to recover them.
2. **Keep source-defined selection separate from accepted targets.** A capacity, each-member predicate, path-generated candidate set, or secondary-beneficiary choice has different meaning. Record known rule conflicts and the table's accepted departure without rewriting source semantics.
3. **Support staged inputs.** Some choices precede dice, some depend on visible dice, some arise while applying damage/movement, and others occur on later turns. “All arguments before roll” and “one invocation is one uninterrupted resolution” both fail concrete cases.
4. **Retain one roll with many contextual results.** Per-target edges/banes, potency comparisons, damage adjustments, conditions, and forced movement may differ. An automatic tier, fixed damage, reroll, and repeated use of an existing roll are distinct facts.
5. **Preserve ordering and dependency.** Damage to all targets normally precedes tier effects; forced moves finish one target at a time; specific abilities override timing. A correction to a source event can invalidate dependent choices/effects, without authorizing unexplained rerolls or duplicate application.
6. **Represent created and lasting entities.** Walls, moving areas, bonds, maintained effects, and squad pools outlive an initial list of target IDs. They need identities that later events and choices can refer to, without prescribing their storage shape here.
7. **Know when geometry is missing.** A map-free client may supply origin, membership, range, line of effect, occupancy, path encounters or chosen destinations as facts. No required digital map is inferred; no absent map is permission to fabricate those facts.
8. **Keep response mechanics within shared operations.** Headless callers need the same pending questions, legal/source-expected choices, recorded answers, and results as action cards. The existing registered-action/card contract supplies the product direction, not a settled timing implementation.

Under the confirmed [rules adaptation principles](../rules-adaptation-principles.md), a **known game-rule conflict warns without blocking eligible play**. An **unknown fact or unsupported mechanic** must remain explicitly unresolved, be supplied, or be manually resolved; it is not a successful automatic calculation. Independent proven effects may proceed, but dependent automation must wait. Full source action text remains available in the log, and applied, manually completed and outstanding work remain distinguishable so resuming does not apply effects twice. These rules do not waive identity, privacy, session lifecycle or persistence-integrity boundaries. This research does not invent routine Director approval.

## Open interpretations and coverage limits

- **Reaction revalidation:** Testudo explicitly moves before damage; it does not fully specify whether a target moved out of the original area remains affected in every possible interaction. Clever Trick and monster substitutions have differing explicit restrictions. A general targeting-lock/recheck policy needs a bounded source review; do not silently extrapolate one ability's exception to all redirections.
- **Modifier scope under reactions:** The sources establish target-local edges/banes and Turnabout's modifier transformations. Their interaction when only some targets have an edge requires precise interpretation; a single global replacement would conceal this issue.
- **Order beyond explicit source statements:** Player-side-first reaction ordering is stated. Universal priority for new triggers generated by an already pending reaction, declaring/withdrawing several competing replacements, and reaction timeouts are not settled here.
- **Spatial corner cases:** Sources identify legal shapes and collision rules, but this note does not certify all three-dimensional placement, size-increment, equal-distance casualty, nearest-space tie, or mixed-size teleport-swap calculations. Keep the supplied facts and adjudication visible.
- **Choice timing where unstated:** Optional target-capacity spends logically affect target capacity, but the general Spend entry does not establish one universal before/after-dice timing window for every optional spend. Only explicit examples such as Again are treated as settled timing facts.
- **Mitigation on transferred damage:** Sacred Bond clearly separates transferred damage from retained associated effects. This survey does not settle every interaction between transfers, multiple defenses, immunity, weakness, and chained transfers. Do not infer applying mitigation twice from the existence of two recipients.
- **Coverage bounds:** Representative cases cover all nine core hero classes, one core kit, three core monster stat blocks and general minion rules. They are selected for distinct input/continuation shapes, not exhaustive ability enumeration. No claim is made for complete conditions, summoned creatures, companions/retainers, mount/tether mechanics, all monster traits/villain actions, negotiation, downtime or respite. Core readability and playable/automated scope remain separate.
